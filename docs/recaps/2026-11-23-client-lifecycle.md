# Session recap, 2026-11-23 (later): the client lifecycle, written and drawn

## What was asked

Taylor asked for the whole client lifecycle at RPB to be written down in one place, from first
contact to a registered mark, and then drawn as a picture the team can walk through. This
followed the morning's read of Caitlyn's SOPs (see
`docs/recaps/2026-11-23-lawmatics-phase-out-sop-read.md`, which is not repeated here).

Nothing in Lawmatics, Lectual or SharePoint was changed.

## What was produced

- **The document:** `docs/rpb/client-lifecycle.md`, 965 lines, committed and pushed on branch
  `claude/eager-fermi-0ff1w7`. It has 30 phases in four stretches, with hand-offs and exits, a
  section on what stalls matters, a Gaps section, a section on Lawmatics while it is still here,
  and a list of decisions the firm needs to make (40 items under eight headings).
- **The visual:** a private page, "RPB Client Lifecycle"
  (https://claude.ai/artifact/9CEapkxwyePAdAPDZ2WhS1). Only Taylor can open it until she shares
  it. One diagram of the 30 phases, four views (who, where it lives, gaps, what the client gets),
  a card per phase, and panels for stalls, hand-offs, disagreements, Lawmatics and decisions.

## How it was done

The work was delegated: six research subagents, one writer, one reviewer and one builder for the
visual. The reviewer read the document twice (19 findings, then 12) and every finding was
applied. Its final judgement: reliable for planning once the fixes were in.

Sources: the three trademark SOPs and the draft SOP for agents; the firm's 44 email and text
templates; the Lawmatics Implementation Guide (a plan, not what is live); team meetings in
Granola from Aug 19 to Nov 17; Taylor's earlier page "SOPs Compared With Lectual" (Nov 17, 257
steps: 26 covered, 108 partly, 88 not in Lectual, 23 different, 12 out of scope by rule); and a
read of the Lectual repository as of Nov 23.

## What was found

Steps carrying each kind of gap: **No written procedure 12. Not in Lectual yet 26. Sources
disagree 18. Done differently in Lectual 21.**

The ten gaps that matter most:

1. **Onboarding has no approved procedure.** The stretch between paying and the search has no
   owner, no times and no checks. Letters have gone unsigned.
2. **A signed proposal opens no matter.** Matters still come from the Lawmatics Hired status, by
   hand, or from an older signed quote.
3. **Money stays in Lawmatics.** Invoices and payments are not in Lectual. Mark paid is a
   stopgap, there is no firm-wide unpaid list, and Dawn cannot see invoices unless she holds the
   owner role.
4. **Nobody reads USPTO notices by itself.** The trademarks@ mailbox is not connected. People
   catch notices, and a missed deadline is the most expensive failure.
5. **A deadline can differ between sources.** TMTKO, the filing receipt and the call can give
   different dates, and no rule says which wins.
6. **The client's part is done by hand.** The Trademark Form with file uploads, form reminders,
   consent and the statement of use form are not in Lectual, so staff move answers across.
7. **Booking and texts are still Lawmatics only.** Lectual has no booking page, no consult fee
   and no text messages, and a no-show looks like a booked consult.
8. **Follow-ups and nurture need a person.** Lectual only drafts. The proposal follow-up is run
   from memory, and no nurture content is loaded.
9. **The order of payment, letter and signing changed three times** between August and
   November. Lectual signs before paying; the intake SOP says pay first.
10. **Every Lectual intake pushes tags to Lawmatics that may start client emails.** Nobody has
    written down which tags do, and nobody has approved those emails.

Also found: about 30 named email and document templates have no drafter in Lectual; there is no
"shot clock" (a time limit per stage that changes colour); the 7-day proceed rule, the stage
numbering and the meeting day differ between documents.

What stalls matters most often (counted from six Monday meetings): waiting on the client's
information (about 20 mentions), no reply to follow-ups (about 12), waiting on Rebecca (about
12), waiting on payment (about 8), waiting on a signature (about 6), waiting on the USPTO
(about 6).

## Recommendation given

Phase-out wording, as it must be read: Taylor's stated direction as of November 23, 2026 is to
phase Lawmatics out. No meeting on record decided it, and whether the rest of the team has been
told is not on record.

Can Lawmatics be organized better to help Lectual? Yes, by tidying data, not structure.

- **Worth doing now:** one record per person; mark hired clients the same way every time
  (status Hired with a conversion date); fill in the matter title or "Name of Trademark"; check
  the "How did you hear about us?" field name against the SOP's "Where did you hear about us?";
  check which tags start a client email.
- **Not worth doing:** renaming stages, rebuilding tags, building the invoice templates and
  reminder sequences the SOPs list, or anything in the old Implementation Guide.
